# ZERO-ID — The Definitive Investor Pitch
### Verbal Script | In-Person | No Slides | 10–12 Minutes
**Setting:** Campus meeting, Startup Foundry Head (JK Cement background)  
**Tone:** Clear, calm, convicted. Simple English. Zero jargon. All business.

---

# How to Use This Script

Read it once, internalize the flow. You don't need to memorize it word-for-word. Know the **structure** and know the **numbers**. The structure is:

```
1. Hook            →  30 seconds   →  Make him lean in
2. Problem         →  90 seconds   →  Make him feel the pain
3. Solution        →  90 seconds   →  Give him the relief
4. Market Size     →  60 seconds   →  Show him how big this is
5. Product & Tech  →  60 seconds   →  Show it's real, not a PPT
6. Business Model  →  90 seconds   →  Show him the money
7. Go-To-Market    →  60 seconds   →  Show him you know how to sell
8. Competition     →  60 seconds   →  Show him you can't be killed
9. Team            →  45 seconds   →  Show him you're the right person
10. The Ask        →  45 seconds   →  Tell him exactly what you need
11. Impact         →  30 seconds   →  Leave him with goosebumps
```

**Total: ~11 minutes. Then Q&A.**

# 1. THE HOOK (30 Seconds)

> "Sir, thank you for your time today. I'll keep this sharp.
>
> Every year in India, **1.2 Billion identity checks** happen — every time someone opens a bank account, applies for a loan, or buys a SIM card.
>
> Every single one of those checks is broken. It costs too much, it's too slow, and it's sitting on a legal time bomb.
>
> We built the fix. It's called **ZERO-ID**."

---

# 2. THE PROBLEM (90 Seconds)

> "Let me give you three numbers that explain why this market is on fire right now.
>
> **Number one: ₹15 to ₹30.**
> That is what a company pays today for every single KYC verification. Whether it's an OCR scan, a Video-KYC call with a live agent, or a DigiLocker API pull — every identity check costs ₹15 to ₹30. For a mid-size lending app processing one lakh applications a month, that is ₹20 to ₹30 Lakhs per month just to verify who their customers are.
>
> **Number two: 40%.**
> That is the onboarding drop-off rate. Customers start a loan application, hit the KYC step — upload your Aadhaar, take a selfie, wait for a Video-KYC agent to call you back — and 40% of them just close the app. Gone. All that customer acquisition cost, wasted.
>
> **Number three: ₹250 Crores.**
> That is the penalty under India's new **Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023**. Section 33. If a company's database gets hacked and customer Aadhaar data leaks, the government can fine them up to **₹250 Crores for a single breach**.
>
> And here is the thing — every company doing KYC today is forced to store copies of their customers' Aadhaar cards on their servers. Photocopies, scanned PDFs, extracted text. **815 million Indian citizens' Aadhaar records were found on the dark web last year.** Companies don't want to store this data. They do it because the old system gives them no choice.
>
> So to summarize the problem: Identity verification in India is **expensive, slow, and legally dangerous**."

---

# 3. THE SOLUTION (90 Seconds)

> "That is why we built **ZERO-ID**.
>
> The simplest way I can explain it: **ZERO-ID is the Apple Pay for identity.**
>
> When you buy a coffee with Apple Pay, you don't hand the shopkeeper your credit card number. Your phone generates a one-time secure token that proves you have money, and the payment goes through in 2 seconds. The shop never sees your card number, never stores it, and if someone hacks the shop's system, there is nothing to steal.
>
> ZERO-ID does the exact same thing, but for your government ID.
>
> The citizen's phone takes their government-signed Aadhaar document, runs a mathematical verification locally on the device, and produces a **cryptographic proof** — a mathematical certificate that says:
> - 'Yes, this person is who they say they are.'
> - 'Yes, they are over 18.'
> - 'Yes, their government signature is authentic.'
>
> The company gets an instant, legally valid, unforgeable verification.
> They pay **₹3 instead of ₹25**.
> The verification takes **1.5 seconds instead of 10 minutes**.
> And they store **zero bytes of personal data** on their servers.
>
> If a hacker breaks into the company's database tomorrow — there is literally nothing there. No names, no photos, no Aadhaar numbers. Zero.
>
> And if a citizen ever loses their phone, they tap one button — our **Kill Switch** — and within 4 seconds, that identity credential is invalidated everywhere in India. No fraudster can ever use it."

# 8. COMPETITION (60 Seconds)

> "Let me address competition head-on.
>
> **Legacy KYC SaaS — HyperVerge, IDfy, Signzy:**
> They charge ₹15–₹25 per check. Their whole model is heavy GPU compute and human manual reviewers in Tier-2 BPOs. If they switch to our model — where verification happens for free on the user's phone — they destroy 80% of their own revenue. That is the classic Innovator's Dilemma. They cannot copy us without cannibalizing themselves.
>
> **DigiLocker / Government APIs:**
> All-or-nothing data dump. Connect to DigiLocker to check if someone is over 18, and it downloads the entire unredacted Aadhaar — full photo, home address, father's name. That directly violates the DPDP Act's data minimization mandate.
>
> **Global Web3 — Worldcoin, Polygon ID:**
> Worldcoin needs a $5,000 eyeball-scanning machine — banned in multiple countries. Polygon ID runs on Ethereum — 12-minute finality and $5–$50 gas fees. We use existing phones and existing Aadhaar cards. Zero proprietary hardware. Instant verification at 8 paise.
>
> **Our moat is 3 layers deep:**
> 1. **Patent-pending cryptographic architecture** — hardware-bound ZK proofs.
> 2. **Hardware biometric binding** — cannot be bolted onto legacy systems.
> 3. **Unit economics so low** that no incumbent can match us without killing their own business."

# DELIVERY RULES

1. **Speak slowly.** Pause after every big number. Let it land. "₹250 Crores..." *(2-second pause)* "...for a single breach."
2. **Use your hands.** Left hand = ₹25 legacy cost. Right hand = ₹3 Zero-ID. Physically show the gap.
3. **Eye contact.** When you say the ask, look him straight in the eye. Don't look down.
4. **The phone demo is your weapon.** Don't ask permission. When you reach Section 5, just smoothly pull it out: "Let me show you something."
5. **JK Cement mention goes in Team, not at the start.** If you say it early, it sounds like name-dropping. In the Team section, it sounds like industrial DNA and fiscal discipline.
6. **If he interrupts with a question mid-pitch — that's a GOOD sign.** He's engaged. Answer it calmly, then return: "Coming back to where I was..."
7. **The closing line matters most.** Don't end with "So yeah, that's about it." End with: *"I would love to have the Startup Foundry as our partner on this journey."* Then stop talking. Let the silence work.
